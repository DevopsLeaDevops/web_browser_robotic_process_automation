ADR 0016: Record runs, and line the video up with the steps by the same clock on replay

The automation stage of validations and executions is recorded with Playwright and kept in the run folder. Steps record the real time they started and ended, and the replay page uses those times to find them in the video. Thumbnails are made in the browser that plays the video.

Accepted

Step timeline and a screenshot per step (2026-10-29)

Updated: 2026-10-29 05:20 America/Los_Angeles (PDT, UTC−07:00). This section amends “thumbnails are made in the browser that plays the video” in decision 6 (“How to replay”) and “a screenshot per step” among the alternatives: the recording remains the main record, and a screenshot per step is added as its thumbnail.

Why: v0.7.0 acceptance (2026-10-28, with a screenshot): thumbnails did not match the timeline or the video, and it was hard to find the step you wanted. The timeline was cut into even tiles, each showing the moment in its middle, with tile edges unrelated to nodes, cards and steps; on a to-scale timeline the steps took a tiny part (13 steps in about 0.65 seconds out of 4.68), the rest being wrap-up after the steps and transition cards; and the video is lined up with the steps only as closely as it is observed (Chromium uses the first observed frame, tests allow ±0.3 seconds) at 40 ms per frame, coarser than fast steps (10–80 ms), so “the frame after a step” taken from the video may show a neighbouring step.

Screenshots: while recording, the runner takes a small JPEG after each step (passed or failed, not skipped) into steps/001.jpg… in the run folder; steps.json gets shot (relative path) and shotAt (when taking it started). Headless Chromium uses DevTools' Page.captureScreenshot (the same command as Playwright's page.screenshot) and has the browser scale it to at most 640 wide; Firefox, WebKit and headed Chromium (a background tab may have no picture, so a timeout is needed) use Playwright's JPEG screenshot (page size, quality 50, at most 2 seconds). A failed screenshot does not affect the step; none is taken with less than 3 seconds left before the deadline; at most 500 per run (a remote worker uploads at most 2000 files per run folder). Step start and end times exclude the screenshot. Measured locally (cloud container, headless Chromium, the 1280×800 demo page): about 30–40 ms more between two steps (the first one sets up the DevTools connection, about 200 ms), 2.4 MB for 200 steps (about 12 KB each). Screenshots are kept with the run record (video retention only deletes videos), remote workers still upload the whole run folder, and like the video they keep everything on screen (the user's D3 of 2026-10-20). The server only accepts file names the runner makes (steps/ plus three or four digits).

Replay: the timeline is laid out “By step” by default: one slot per step (at least 44 px, extra width shared by the square root of each step's length), stretches of more than 0.5 seconds without a step in the same recording folded into a 20 px slot, and 40 px for each card. Time and position are converted piecewise-linearly, used by the playhead, dragging, hover preview, ticks (showing real times) and bands. Thumbnails are the step screenshots; without one (older runs) replay falls back to the video frame 3 frames after the step ended, and video thumbnails are cached per frame (25 per second). Playback runs at 4× through folded stretches except their last 0.4 seconds (clicking a step jumps 0.4 seconds before it); only in the step view, and it can be turned off in the controls. When a step cannot get 44 px, slots get narrower and several steps share a thumbnail; a single run is split into groups of steps and a composite run into its nodes, and clicking one zooms in. “By time” switches back to the earlier to-scale timeline, and the browser remembers the choice. The step list and the details use the same screenshot, which survives the video's deletion. The step details now slide down above the list without covering it (the user's change of 2026-10-29; on 2026-10-28 they slid in from the right over the list).

Not done: estimating and correcting the video's timing offset from the screenshots (marked “if feasible” in the design proposal). Thumbnails no longer depend on the video's timing; estimating the offset means comparing pictures, which page animations, the cursor and compression noise easily mislead, and a wrong estimate would make the video less accurate. It will be reconsidered with a real case. Making thumbnails also does not wait for requestVideoFrameCallback: the video used for thumbnails is not on screen, so the callback may never come; it seeks to frame times instead.

Implementation and verification: see the task handoff.

Multi-page and timing correction (2026-10-22)

Updated: 2026-10-22 00:35 America/Los_Angeles (PDT, UTC−07:00). This section supersedes the earlier assumptions that one video covers a run and that out-of-range steps should remain on its final frame.

Cause: Playwright records each page separately. Finalization previously kept only the main video and removed other files with the temporary folder. Successful steps do not mean the main video contains popup activity. Replay also attributed every step beyond that video's duration to an interrupted run.

Storage: video.json v2 adds clips with pageId, file, startedAt, startedAtSource, durationMs, dimensions and completeness. The initial page uses video.webm; other pages use video-page-N.webm. If the main clip is unavailable, the first usable clip retains the compatible video.webm entry point. Original top-level fields still describe that primary clip; timelineStartedAt and timelineDurationMs describe total coverage. steps.json adds pageId and createdPageId. Remote workers upload the whole run directory; quota accounting and removal cover all clips.

Replay: Steps and clips use a shared UTC-based timeline. Playback, seeking and thumbnails select a clip by page identity, including returns to the initial page. Overlapping recordings are not concatenated. Loading pauses the clock; missing, corrupt and uncovered intervals show a gap. Legacy single videos remain readable, but deleted popup footage cannot be reconstructed. Downloads provide each original page recording, not a single rendered composite.

Chromium timing: Synthetic tests on local Playwright 1.64 showed a zero first-frame PTS while DateUTC differed from the actual picture origin. The runner immediately subscribes to the public Screencast API, records the first observed frame timestamp and unsubscribes. When the file's first PTS is zero, this observed origin is used with source first-observed-frame. This is an observed baseline, not a frame-exact guarantee. Tests compare colors 0.3 seconds before and after clicks. Failed observation retains the file/page fallback; old recordings receive no fixed offset. The minimum Playwright version becomes 1.64.

Validation scope: Synthetic white, blue and red pages cover headed/headless recording, popup closure and return, continuous playback, step jumps, missing clips, cancellation/timeouts and retention. This change does not address Firefox sizing/flicker or rerun business transactions. Windows, Linux and deployed product results depend on CI and post-deployment acceptance.

Public reference: Playwright video storage and multi-page behavior. Implementation evidence and test results: task handoff.

Context

When a run fails, the report only has the last screenshot, the step results and the logs, so you cannot see what happened on the screen before the failure. Runs usually happen on another worker, and in headless mode nothing is visible at all. The user asked (2026-10-20) for validations and executions to be recorded, headed or headless; for the recording to be replayed from the run record after the run; and for replay to be a separate, frequently used feature that can pause, seek, find the moment by thumbnails and follow the steps, so the run can be reconstructed.

Playwright can record video on its own: the browser hands every frame and the real time it appeared to Playwright, which encodes them into WebM with its own ffmpeg.

Decision

  1. What is recorded. The automation stage of DSL scenarios, using BrowserContext recording, headed or headless; the video is as large as the page, at most 1280 wide (scaled down proportionally). Login checks, step-by-step debugging, recording sessions and Python script scenarios are not recorded. Firefox's warm profile starts with a tab already open, and that tab records frames missing the height of the toolbar (measured on Windows and Linux), so recording uses a newly opened tab instead.
  2. How it lines up with the steps. Playwright encodes every frame at its real time, so second t of the video is the recording start time plus t; the start time is in the WebM header (DateUTC, milliseconds). steps.json records each step's startedAt and endedAt (UTC, milliseconds) and the page URL after it. Both come from the same clock on the same computer, so subtracting gives the step's place in the video. If the header has no start time (older Playwright), the time the page was opened is used.
  3. How it is finished. The subprocess closes the BrowserContext before it exits, which is when Playwright finishes the video; the parent turns it into video.webm and video.json (start time, length, size, whether it was repaired). When the subprocess is terminated (cancelled, or past the overall deadline): on Linux and macOS Playwright runs ffmpeg in its own process group, so it is not terminated along with the rest and finishes the file itself; on Windows the process tree cannot simply be ended: taskkill /T would end ffmpeg too, and keeping only ffmpeg is not enough either, because Node (Playwright's driver) puts the processes it starts in a Job Object that ends them all when Node ends. So while recording, the runner lists the descendants first and ends only the browsers: Playwright finishes each page's video as usual, and the subprocess exits by itself because its page closed (0.1 to 0.3 seconds in CI); only if it has not exited within 15 seconds is the whole process tree ended. How the run was stopped is recorded under stop in video.json. A video that still has no length or index is remuxed with Playwright's own ffmpeg (no re-encoding).
  4. Where it is kept. In the run folder (remote workers still upload the whole folder); the runs table records the length, size, pin, and when and why the video was deleted. Videos do not go into domain folders or git.
  5. How long it is kept. A server setting; by default everything is recorded, passed runs keep the video for 7 days, and failed, timed-out, cancelled and needs-login runs for 30 days; all videos together use at most 20 GB, deleting the oldest first; pinned videos are kept. Clean-up only deletes videos: run records, reports and screenshots stay, and run.json records when and why the video was deleted.
  6. How it is replayed. The replay page plays the video straight from the run folder (with Range support and caching). Thumbnails are made in the browser that plays the video, by seeking a hidden video and drawing it to a canvas; the Linux server has neither a browser nor ffmpeg.

Consequences

  • About 1–3 MB per minute (1280 wide; less when the screen is still). The retention rules and the total limit keep disk use in check.
  • Workers run an extra real-time ffmpeg encoder (VP9; Playwright adjusts threads and bitrate to the frame size).
  • The video keeps everything on the screen, including business data and values typed into ordinary fields. The user agreed to keep them (2026-10-20, D3); until M8, anyone who can open the management portal can see them.
  • Cancelling while recording waits on Windows for the subprocess to exit by itself (usually under a second, at most 15 seconds). Actions still stop at the same moment: the browsers are ended right away.
  • The browser has to play WebM VP9: Chrome, Edge and Firefox do; Safari on macOS and the recorder app (WKWebView) need manual acceptance.
  • A terminated video only covers up to the last change on the screen; the waiting after that has no frames. The replay page attaches steps beyond the end of the video to its last frame.
